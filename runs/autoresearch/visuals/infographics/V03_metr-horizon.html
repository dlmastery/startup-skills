Ascent · V03 · Audience: VC

Execution is solved; judgment is the gap — we ship the judgment layer

The enabling capability arrived in the last ~18 months, and it arrived lopsided: agents now out-execute humans in short bursts and lose badly over long horizons. That asymmetry is the entire product thesis.
Zone 1 — Task horizon
~12 hours @ 50% reliability, doubling every ~4 months
mid-2026 — the multi-hour experiment loop did not exist as a dependable capability 18 months ago [B2]

Execution is theirs; judgment is ours. Ascent does not bet on agents getting better at long-horizon judgment — it supplies the judgment as an enforced protocol and lets agents do the part they already win. [B4]
Zone 3 — MLE-bench: medaling, not correctness
16.9% → 40–65%
Oct 2024 → 2026. Best published full-MLE-bench medal rates span a range, not a cherry-picked ceiling: Operand Quant 39.6% [A10], ML-ACE ~56% [B13], MLEvolve 65.3% at a 12-hour budget [D39].
The gap that stays open: agents routinely medal, and unsupervised correctness is still unsolved. Medaling is scored against a held-out leaderboard someone else built — nobody grades the agent's own evaluation protocol. That is the job Ascent takes.
Zone 4 — The cost curve makes it a line item, not a project
~40–50× / yr
fall in cost per unit of model capability — yesterday's frontier becomes cheap mid-tier fast [B16][B18][B22]
$3–12 / day
mid-tier-routed 24/7 steering — under 10% of one ML engineer's loaded daily cost. All-frontier routing would run $20–60/day: the frontier slice is deliberately thin.
Stated honestly: frontier list prices are flat-to-rising. The 40–50× is capability-per-dollar, not sticker price — which is exactly why model routing is a margin feature and not an optimization detail.
Zone 5 — Why now, in one line
Horizons doubled every ~4 months, agents win short sprints 4×, and capability cost falls 40–50×/yr — so the sustained overnight campaign became buildable, and the only thing still missing is the referee.

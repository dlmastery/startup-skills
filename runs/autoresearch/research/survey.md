# Autonomous ML Research Systems: A Mini Survey of the Literature Behind Constitution-Driven, Audit-Gated Hill-Climbing

**Date:** 2026-08-27 · **Prepared for:** the Ascent artifact pack · **Status:** internal research synthesis; every claim carries a source tag [Dn] resolved in the Sources section. All papers were verified to exist via live web search on the date above.

---

## Abstract

Between 2015 and 2026 the automation of machine-learning research proceeded in three distinguishable waves: (1) classical AutoML, which automated hyperparameter and architecture search but nothing upstream of it [D1–D4]; (2) agentic "AI scientist" systems (2024–2026), which use LLM agents to run the full idea-to-paper pipeline but were shown by independent evaluation to fail frequently at execution and to be unable to audit their own results [D13–D21]; and (3) metric-grinding agent loops — AIDE-style tree search on MLE-bench and, most visibly, Karpathy's autoresearch ratchet (March 2026) — which optimize a fixed measurable objective through repeated keep-or-discard experiments [D22–D24, D39]. In parallel, a separate literature documented why unaudited automated experimentation is dangerous: data leakage affects at least 294 published papers across 17 fields [D5]; unrejected multiple testing inflates backtest Sharpe ratios so reliably that quantitative finance developed dedicated corrections (deflated Sharpe ratio, purged/embargoed cross-validation) [D6–D8]; and any proxy metric under optimization pressure is subject to Goodhart effects that provably cannot be fully designed away [D30, D31]. This survey reviews all four strands, tabulates the current evaluation benchmarks and their best scores, examines the published evidence for and against the four mechanisms Ascent builds on (literature grounding, monotonic keep/discard, leakage-proof evaluation, programmatic audit gates), and closes with an honest placement of the Ascent proof of concept: it occupies a real and documented gap — the rigor layer for long-horizon metric-grinding loops — but its results are founder-reported, single-seed in most domains, and not yet peer-reviewed or independently reproduced.

## 1. Classical foundations

### 1.1 AutoML: automating the last mile

The modern AutoML lineage begins with sequential model-based (Bayesian) optimization of hyperparameters — SMAC and its successors [D3] — and matured into full pipeline search with **auto-sklearn** (Feurer et al., NeurIPS 2015; Auto-sklearn 2.0, JMLR 2022), which combined Bayesian optimization with meta-learning warm starts and ensembling [D1]. **BOHB** (Falkner, Klein & Hutter, ICML 2018) fused Bayesian optimization with Hyperband's multi-fidelity early stopping [D2], and joint neural-architecture-search-plus-HPO systems such as Auto-PyTorch extended the paradigm to deep learning [D4]. Commercial descendants (AutoGluon, H2O AutoML, DataRobot, SageMaker Autopilot) industrialized this recipe.

What this entire lineage automates is the *final* stage of research: given a fixed dataset, fixed features, fixed evaluation protocol, and fixed metric, search a bounded configuration space. It does not read literature, does not diagnose *why* a model underperforms, does not design evaluation protocols, and does not guard against leakage in the user's data preparation — the steps that consume most practitioner time. This boundary is precisely where the agentic wave (§2) and Ascent position themselves.

### 1.2 The reproducibility and leakage literature

The strongest empirical warning about automating experimentation without safeguards comes from **Kapoor & Narayanan (Patterns, 2023)** [D5], who surveyed ML-based science and found data leakage documented in **17 fields, affecting at least 294 papers**, in some cases overturning headline conclusions (their civil-war-prediction case study: after leakage correction, ML models no longer beat logistic regression). They contribute an eight-type leakage taxonomy ranging from textbook errors (no train/test split, preprocessing on the full dataset) to subtle temporal and non-independence leakage, and propose "model info sheets" as a preregistration-like remedy. **Leech et al. (arXiv:2407.12220)** [D10] catalogue **44 questionable research practices** in ML — grouped into contamination, cherry-picking, and misreporting — that fall short of fraud but inflate reported results. Complementing these, **Bouthillier et al. (MLSys 2021, arXiv:2103.03098)** [D11] show that variance from data sampling, initialization, and hyperparameter choice is large enough to reorder leaderboards, with bootstrap-induced data variance the single largest component; **Picard (arXiv:2109.08203)** [D12] found that simply scanning random seeds moves CIFAR-10 accuracy by margins larger than many published "improvements." Together these papers define the failure surface that any automated experimenter multiplies: an agent running hundreds of experiments per day commits these errors at machine speed unless structurally prevented.

### 1.3 Backtest overfitting in quantitative finance — the direct ancestry of Ascent's evaluation design

Quantitative finance confronted automated-search overfitting a decade before the agent era, and its solutions are the direct intellectual ancestors of Ascent's purge/embargo super-folds. **Bailey & López de Prado's Deflated Sharpe Ratio** (Journal of Portfolio Management, 2014) corrects a reported Sharpe ratio for selection bias under multiple testing and non-normal returns: given the number of trials actually run, it computes the Sharpe one should expect from pure noise and deflates accordingly [D6]. **Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu** formalized the **Probability of Backtest Overfitting** (Journal of Computational Finance, 2015), showing that with enough strategy variants tested, a high in-sample Sharpe is nearly guaranteed and out-of-sample performance is often *negatively* related to in-sample performance [D7]. **López de Prado's *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* (Wiley, 2018)** introduced the operational fixes now standard in the field: **purged cross-validation** (drop training observations whose labels overlap the test window), **embargo** (additionally drop a buffer after the test window to stop serial-correlation leakage), and **combinatorial purged CV** (many purged train/test paths yielding a distribution of out-of-sample estimates rather than a point) [D8]. Ascent's "super-folds with purge/embargo buffers" descend from this line, and its 265-experiment EUR/USD run is exactly the multiple-testing regime the deflated Sharpe ratio was invented for — a point we return to in §6 and §7.

Outside finance, **Gelman & Loken's "garden of forking paths"** (2014) generalizes the same problem: data-dependent analytic choices create implicit multiple comparisons that inflate false positives even without deliberate p-hacking [D9]. An autonomous agent choosing its next experiment *based on the last result* is a forking-paths machine by construction.

## 2. Taxonomy of automated-research approaches

The literature to date sorts into six families. The table orders them roughly by how much of the research loop they automate and what they optimize.

| Family | Representative systems | What is automated | Objective | Rigor machinery | Hardware regime | Documented weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bayesian/multi-fidelity AutoML | auto-sklearn [D1], BOHB [D2], SMAC [D3], AutoGluon/H2O | Model/hyperparameter/pipeline search | Fixed metric on fixed data | CV inside search; nothing upstream | Laptop→cluster | Automates only the last mile; blind to leakage in data prep; no literature awareness |
| One-shot paper generators | AI Scientist v1/v2 [D13, D14], Agent Laboratory [D15], MLR-Copilot [D16], CodeScientist [D19] | Idea → experiments → written paper | Paper acceptance / reviewer score | LLM self-review (unreliable, §4.4) | Cloud API budgets | 42% of experiments failed on coding errors in independent eval; cannot assess own results [D20]; weak novelty checking [D20, D21] |
| Hypothesis co-pilots | Google Co-Scientist [D18], SciMON [D29] | Hypothesis generation, ranking, evolution | Expert-rated novelty/quality | Tournament debate; human-in-loop | Cloud | Stops at hypothesis; humans run the experiments |
| Metric-grinding agent loops | AIDE [D23], MLE-bench top agents [D24, D39], Karpathy autoresearch [D22] | Iterative code change → run → keep/discard | Fixed measurable metric | Held-out score as sole gate | Cloud (AIDE) or single GPU (autoresearch) | No leakage protection, no literature grounding, no audit trail; vulnerable to metric gaming (§4.2) |
| Collaborative/evolutionary hybrids | AgentRxiv [D17], DeepEvolve [D28] | Multi-agent sharing of prior results; research-guided code evolution | Benchmark accuracy / algorithm quality | Shared memory of validated results | Cloud | Early stage; evaluation mostly on public benchmarks with contamination risk (§6) |
| Rigor-enforcement architectures | Sargsyan's Research-monad/FDR architecture [D32]; Ascent PoC (this pack) | Statistical bookkeeping around any of the above | Valid discoveries at controlled error rate | Type-system/sandbox-enforced testing budgets; audit gates | Laptop-viable | Newest and least validated family; Sargsyan is architecture-only, Ascent is founder-reported |

Two observations follow. First, the fourth row (metric-grinding loops) is the only family that does what Ascent's users want — grind a fixed real-world metric upward over days — and it is the family with the *least* rigor machinery: its sole safeguard is the held-out score itself, which §4.2 shows is exactly the thing long-running optimization corrupts. Second, the sixth row (structural rigor enforcement) barely exists: Sargsyan (arXiv:2511.06701, Nov 2025) is, to our knowledge, the first published architecture making uncontrolled hypothesis testing *impossible* rather than discouraged — via a typed research monad that debits an FDR error budget on every test and an OS-level sandbox that keeps validation data physically absent from the agent's execution environment [D32]. That paper is an architecture with formal proofs, not an evaluated system; the gap between rows four and six is the space Ascent claims.

## 3. Enabling technology

Three capability trends made this field possible, all post-2024.

**Long-horizon coding agents.** MLE-bench's launch result (October 2024) — o1-preview with AIDE scaffolding earning a Kaggle-medal-equivalent on 16.9% of 75 competitions [D24] — was the first quantified evidence that LLM agents could run multi-step ML engineering unattended. By mid-2026 the public MLE-bench leaderboard had moved dramatically: the MLEvolve system reports a **65.3% medal rate on the full 75-task benchmark at a 12-hour budget (June 2026)** [D39], while the llm-stats leaderboard tracker showed a top score of 0.639 (Gemini 3.6 Flash, August 2026) [D39] — the two trackers use different scaffolds and budgets, so we report both rather than reconcile them. METR's RE-Bench [D26] found agents beat human experts 4× at 2-hour budgets on frontier-lab research-engineering tasks, with humans showing better returns to time at longer horizons — evidence that agent advantage is currently strongest in exactly the many-short-experiments regime a hill-climbing loop uses.

**Falling inference cost.** AI Scientist v1 reported full papers at under **$15 in API cost** [D13]; Agent Laboratory reported an ~84% cost reduction relative to prior autonomous pipelines [D15]. Multi-day 24/7 agent experimentation on consumer hardware is economically routine as of 2026 — the premise of both Karpathy's autoresearch and Ascent.

**Proof of demand for the minimal loop.** Karpathy's **autoresearch** (released March 7, 2026) gave an agent a ~630-line single-GPU nanochat training script and a ratchet rule — run 5-minute training jobs, commit only improvements — and accumulated 66,000+ stars and ~9,600 forks within a month [D22] (the founder brief cites 78k+ stars as of August 2026; we could not independently confirm the August figure). Public analyses of the fork ecosystem describe mostly hardware ports and prompt tweaks rather than rigor extensions [D22], consistent with the brief's claim that the rigor layer is the unfilled gap — though we note this characterization of 9,600 forks is based on commentary, not an exhaustive audit.

## 4. Evidence on the core mechanisms

Ascent's thesis decomposes into four mechanisms. Each has a real literature; none of the literatures is uniformly favorable.

### 4.1 Literature grounding: does grounding search in prior work beat blind search?

**Evidence for.** The strongest direct experiment is **DeepEvolve (arXiv:2510.06056)** [D28], which augments AlphaEvolve-style pure code evolution with a deep-research retrieval step. Its motivating finding: evolution driven only by the LLM's internal knowledge "quickly plateaus in complex domains," while retrieval-grounded iteration produced sustained gains across nine benchmarks in chemistry, mathematics, biology, and materials. **AgentRxiv (arXiv:2503.18102)** [D17] found agents with access to prior (agent-generated) research achieved an 11.4% relative improvement on MATH-500 over isolated agents, rising to 13.7% when multiple labs shared results — grounding in *accumulated validated results*, the same mechanism as Ascent's winner archive. **SciMON** [D29] showed retrieval from literature plus explicit iterative novelty comparison measurably improves idea quality over ungrounded GPT-4 generation. Google's Co-Scientist [D18] similarly attributes its expert-preferred hypotheses to grounding in retrieved prior evidence plus tournament evolution.

**Evidence against / caveats.** Grounding is only as good as the citations are real: fabricated-reference rates in ungrounded LLM generation run from ~18–20% (GPT-4/GPT-4o) to 47–77% for reference titles in some domains, and 14–95% across 13 models depending on field [D35, D36]; Beel et al. found the AI Scientist's literature reviews misclassified textbook concepts as novel [D20]. So the mechanism works *if and only if* retrieval is verified — which is an argument for a citation-rigor audit gate (§4.4), not against grounding. Net read: **directionally well-supported**, with DeepEvolve the cleanest head-to-head.

### 4.2 Monotonic keep/discard hill-climbing: properties and failure modes

**Evidence for.** Greedy keep-if-better search is the engine of every system that currently tops the agent benchmarks: AIDE's solution-tree search, which "strategically reuses and refines promising solutions," achieved SOTA on MLE-bench at launch [D23, D24], and its successors held the 2026 leaderboard [D39]. Karpathy's ratchet demonstrated the same loop works unattended overnight on a single GPU [D22]. The mechanism's virtues are real: no regression persists, progress is legible, and each accepted step is individually testable.

**Evidence against.** Three documented failure modes:

1. **Goodhart / metric corruption.** Skalse et al. (NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2209.13085) prove that for any non-trivial proxy reward there is no guarantee against hacking — unhackable proxies essentially don't exist [D30]. Karwowski et al. (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.09144) show optimization pressure on a proxy characteristically *increases* true performance up to a threshold and then degrades it [D31] — a precise description of what happens to a composite metric ground on for 265 experiments. A monotonic ratchet guarantees monotonicity *in the proxy*, not in the truth.
2. **Selection bias under multiple testing.** A keep-if-better rule applied to N noisy evaluations is a max-of-N selection: with single-seed evaluations and hundreds of trials, some "improvements" are pure seed luck [D11, D12], and in finance this is quantified — expected maximum in-sample Sharpe grows with trials even under a true Sharpe of zero [D6, D7]. The mitigation literature is exactly §1.3's: deflate for trial count, demand distributional (multi-path) evidence, hold out a final untouched test set.
3. **Local optima and plateaus.** Pure greedy evolution plateaus without externally injected ideas [D28] — which is the coupling argument for §4.1: literature grounding is what re-seeds the search when hill-climbing stalls.

Net read: hill-climbing is the **demonstrated best-performing search strategy** in this domain, and simultaneously the mechanism most in need of the other three safeguards. The honest claim is not "monotonic search is safe" but "monotonic search is only safe when the metric is leakage-proof, trial-count-aware, and audit-gated."

### 4.3 Leakage-proof evaluation: purged/embargoed splits

This is the best-evidenced mechanism of the four, because it imports a mature literature rather than a new idea. Purging and embargoing are standard practice in financial ML [D8], implemented in maintained open-source libraries (e.g., skfolio's CombinatorialPurgedCV) and described in a dedicated Wikipedia entry — i.e., settled methodology, not a research bet. Kapoor & Narayanan's 294-paper leakage census [D5] establishes the size of the problem the mechanism prevents, and their taxonomy doubles as a test suite for a data-integrity gate. Two caveats keep this honest: (a) purge/embargo addresses *temporal/overlap* leakage; several of the eight Kapoor–Narayanan leakage types (e.g., feature legitimacy, sampling bias) are semantic and cannot be caught by split geometry alone — they need the audit gate layer; (b) leakage-proof splits do nothing about multiple-testing inflation (§4.2), so "structurally impossible leakage" must not be marketed as "structurally impossible overfitting." The founder brief's phrase "leakage structurally impossible" should be scoped to the leakage classes the folds actually cover.

### 4.4 Programmatic audit gates

**Evidence that gates are needed.** Independent evaluation of the AI Scientist (Beel et al., arXiv:2502.14297) found **42% of its experiments failed on coding errors**, novelty assessments were poor, and — the central finding — the system "cannot critically assess its own results and fails to detect methodological flaws," making unaudited output potentially misleading [D20]. Trehan & Chopra (arXiv:2601.03315) ran four end-to-end autonomous paper attempts (three failed) and distilled six recurring failure modes, including "overexcitement that declares success despite obvious failures" and implementation drift [D21]. These are exactly the failures a pre-commit gate on data integrity, citation validity, and reasoning completeness is designed to catch.

**Evidence on whether gates work.** Mixed, and mechanism-dependent. LLM-judge-based gates inherit documented unreliability: large-scale meta-evaluations find judge models reliable but not valid — consistent scores that don't track ground truth — with systematic biases [D33], and cross-model agreement is shown to be a confidence signal that can reflect shared bias rather than correctness [D34]. By contrast, *deterministic* gates have strong support: citation verification against live indexes reduces fabricated references to near zero in retrieval-verified pipelines (the entire premise of the reference-hallucination-detection literature [D35, D36]), and Sargsyan [D32] demonstrates that statistical-validity gates can be made *structural* — machine-checked FDR accounting where testing without debiting the error budget is a type error, plus sandbox-level absence of validation data. The design implication for Ascent: gates that check mechanically verifiable properties (does the citation exist; was the test set touched; does the hash match; was the error budget debited) rest on solid evidence; gates that ask an LLM to judge "reasoning completeness" rest on the shakiest part of the literature and should be treated as a screen, not a guarantee.

## 5. Evaluation methods and benchmarks

| Benchmark | What it measures | Scale | Best published results | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MLE-bench** (OpenAI, arXiv:2410.07095, ICLR 2025) [D24] | Kaggle-style end-to-end ML engineering; medal-equivalent thresholds | 75 competitions | Launch: o1-preview+AIDE, 16.9% medal rate. 2026: MLEvolve reports 65.3% (12 h, June 2026); llm-stats tracker top score 0.639 (Aug 2026) [D39] | Contamination from pre-training explicitly studied by authors; leaderboard entries use heterogeneous compute budgets |
| **MLAgentBench** (arXiv:2310.03302) [D25] | Iterative ML experimentation on 13 tasks (CIFAR-10 → BabyLM) | 13 tasks | Claude 3 Opus agent, 37.5% average success | Small task count; success ≠ SOTA improvement |
| **RE-Bench** (METR, arXiv:2411.15114) [D26] | Frontier AI R&D engineering vs. 61 human experts, 71 8-hour attempts | 7 environments | Agents 4× human score at 2-h budget; human returns to time are steeper at long horizons | Deliberately hard, frontier-lab-flavored tasks; small N |
| **MLRC-Bench** (arXiv:2504.09702) [D27] | Solving open ML *research* competition problems, objective metrics | Rolling | Agents close only a small fraction of the human-SOTA gap (paper's central finding) | Newer, less adopted |

Methodological notes for anyone citing these numbers. First, medal-rate comparisons across time conflate model, scaffold, and budget improvements. Second, all four benchmarks score *final metric achieved*, not *validity of process* — none would detect a leaky split or an overfit selection, which is why a system can top MLE-bench while flunking the §1.2 literature's standards. A process-validity benchmark does not yet exist (open question, §6). Third, paper-generator systems are evaluated differently — human/LLM review of outputs (AI Scientist-v2 achieved the first fully AI-generated peer-review-accepted workshop paper, at ICLR 2025 workshop level [D14]; one of Trehan & Chopra's four attempts passed review at Agents4Science 2025 [D21]) — and workshop acceptance is a weak, noisy signal of scientific validity, as those authors themselves note.

## 6. Risks and open questions

**Multiple comparisons and the garden of forking paths, at machine speed.** An autonomous experimenter is a forking-paths generator [D9]: every diagnose→hypothesize step conditions on observed data. Finance solved this for its domain with trial-count-aware statistics [D6, D7]; ML broadly has not, and Sargsyan's FDR architecture [D32] is the only published structural solution we found. **Open question:** should a keep/discard loop's acceptance threshold rise with experiment count (a deflated-composite-metric analogue)? No published system does this today, Ascent's PoC included.

**Seed and sampling variance.** With single-seed evaluations, a ratchet will accumulate some seed-lucky "improvements" [D11, D12]. Distributional evaluation (CPCV-style multi-path [D8], multi-seed) is the known fix and is compute-expensive — in tension with laptop-native operation.

**Benchmark contamination.** Contamination of public benchmarks in LLM training data is widespread — detected at up to 45% on popular benchmarks and higher in some multilingual suites [D37, D38] — which taints any agent validation done on public tasks (Higgs, PathMNIST, Kaggle fraud data included): the agent's underlying LLM may "know" published solutions. Private, post-cutoff datasets are the clean test; this cuts *for* Ascent's user-owned-data use case and *against* over-reading its public-benchmark PoC results.

**Goodharting the composite metric.** §4.2's threshold result [D31] implies long runs need metric-integrity monitoring (e.g., tracking divergence between composite proxy and each raw target metric). SHA-256 fingerprinting makes the metric *tamper-evident* but not *Goodhart-proof* — it freezes the proxy; it cannot make the proxy true.

**LLM-judged gates.** Any gate implemented as "LLM judges the reasoning" inherits the reliability-without-validity problem [D33, D34]. Gate design should be pushed toward deterministic checks wherever possible.

**Human oversight economics.** Every independent evaluation to date [D20, D21] concludes current systems need human oversight. The open question for the category is not "can the loop run alone" but "what is the minimum steering bandwidth" — Ascent's steering-log evidence is one data point; nobody has published a measurement.

## 7. Where the Ascent PoC sits in this literature

**What the literature genuinely supports.** (1) The gap is real: the best-performing loop family (metric-grinding, §2 row 4) demonstrably lacks the rigor layer, and the rigor-architecture family (§2 row 6) demonstrably lacks evaluated systems — Ascent's positioning between them matches the published record, not just founder framing. (2) Each of the four mechanisms is individually grounded: literature-grounded search beats blind search in the cleanest available head-to-head [D28]; keep/discard search is the current benchmark-topping strategy [D23, D24, D39]; purge/embargo evaluation is settled financial-ML methodology [D8]; and the failure modes audit gates target are exactly those independent evaluators documented [D20, D21]. (3) Laptop-native long-horizon operation was proven viable at community scale by autoresearch [D22]. Ascent's bet — that the *composition* of these four mechanisms is the product — is a synthesis no published system has evaluated, which is both its novelty claim and its evidentiary gap.

**What remains unproven.** All PoC results — EUR/USD test Sharpe +6.52 over 265+ experiments, Higgs AUROC 0.8675+, PathMNIST OOD 0.997, Olivetti ARI 0.874, beating AutoGluon/H2O on fraud detection — are **founder-reported, not peer-reviewed, and not independently reproduced**. Specific open exposures, stated so the rest of this pack cannot overstate them: (a) a Sharpe selected as the champion of 265+ trials is precisely the statistic the deflated Sharpe ratio exists to discount [D6, D7]; until the DSR (with the true trial count) or a CPCV path distribution [D8] is reported, +6.52 is an upper bound on the honest number, and a raw Sharpe of that magnitude would place the strategy far outside published norms — extraordinary-claims territory. (b) Most PoC domains use public datasets subject to the contamination concern [D37, D38]. (c) The audit gates' *catch rates* (true/false positive rates against seeded violations) are unmeasured — the literature says such gates are needed [D20, D21] and that structural versions are buildable [D32], not that Ascent's three gates work. (d) Multi-seed variance [D11, D12] is unreported for most champions. The riskiest-assumption test in the founder brief (15 practitioners, one-command reproduction) is well-aimed: independent reproduction is the single action that would move every result above from "founder-reported" to "evidence," and the purge/embargo + fingerprint + winner-archive design is, correctly, the machinery that makes such reproduction cheap.

**Bottom line.** The literature of 2024–2026 converged on two findings that jointly define this company's opportunity: autonomous agents can now grind ML metrics better than humans over short horizons [D24, D26, D39], and no one should trust what they produce without structural safeguards [D5, D20, D21, D30]. Ascent is a bet that the second finding is a product. The science says the ingredients work; the system-level claim awaits independent validation.

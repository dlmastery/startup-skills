## 1. Long-horizon autonomous coding — what changed in the last 18 months

1. **The time-horizon curve is the cleanest "why now" evidence that exists.** METR's 50%-time-horizon metric (task length a human would need, that the agent completes 50% of the time) was ~50 minutes for Claude 3.7 Sonnet (early 2025), 5.3 h for Claude Opus 4.5 (TH1.1, Jan 29 2026), ~6.6 h estimated for GPT-5.2, and ~12 h at the frontier by Apr–May 2026 [B1][B2]. Doubling time compressed from ~7 months (2019–2025 average) to ~4 months since 2024 [B1][B3]. In early 2024 the same metric was well under 30 minutes — i.e., the multi-hour autonomous experiment loop **did not exist as a reliable capability 18 months ago**, exactly as the brief claims.
2. **Vendor and telemetry corroboration.** Anthropic claimed 30 h of continuous autonomous coding for Sonnet 4.5 (Sep 29 2025; an enterprise trial reportedly built an app, stood up databases, and ran a SOC 2 audit unattended) [B5][B6]. Anthropic's own telemetry shows Claude Code's 99.9th-percentile turn duration nearly doubling from <25 min to >45 min between Oct 2025 and Jan 2026 [B7]. OpenAI reports median Codex agentic turns of ~2.5 h by Jun 2026 [B10a]. Cognition reports Devin's PR merge rate rising 34% → 67% year-over-year [B10b].
3. **Runtime maturity.** The field consolidated to ~5 production-grade agent harnesses (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Replit Agent, Devin), and the *engineering patterns* for long-running agents — checkpointing, memory compaction, harness design — are now published openly by Anthropic (Nov 2025, Mar 2026 guides) [B8][B10]. Ascent doesn't have to invent the runtime; it rides it.
4. **Caveats worth keeping honest.** SWE-bench Verified at ~95% (Fable 5, Aug 2026) is impressive but the benchmark is heavily exposed in training data and audits urge contamination caveats [B9]. And 50%-reliability at 12 h is *not* multi-day unattended reliability — which is why Ascent's chunked, checkpointed, audit-gated loop (many short verified experiments rather than one heroic 72-h run) is the architecturally correct consumption of this capability.

## 2. Autonomous ML-research benchmarks — what agents can and can't do

5. **MLE-bench (OpenAI, 75 Kaggle competitions):** best published medal rate went from 16.9% (o1-preview + AIDE, Oct 2024) [B11] to ~39.6% (Operand Quant) [B12], ~56.4% (ML-ACE), 75.8%/50.9%/42.2% on Low/Medium/High complexity (ML-Master 2.0, 2026) [B13], with one 2026 system (EurekAgent) reporting 85.7% on a subset [B13a]. Translation: on well-posed tabular/vision/NLP problems with a fixed metric, agents now *routinely* produce medal-grade solutions — the exact regime of Ascent's hill-climbing loop.
6. **RE-bench (METR, 7 open-ended ML research-engineering environments):** best agents score ~4× human experts at 2-h budgets, but humans overtake at 8 h and reach ~2× the best agent at 32-h budgets (Nov 2024) [B4]. This is the sharpest published statement of the division of labor Ascent assumes: agents dominate short, well-specified experiment execution; humans retain the advantage in long-horizon research judgment. Ascent's "human steers, constitution enforces judgment, agent executes" is the direct product-shaped answer to this curve.
7. **MLAgentBench / DSBench:** MLAgentBench measures whether agents achieve ≥10% improvement over given baselines across 13 ML tasks; DSBench (466 analysis + 74 modeling tasks) found agents far from human data-science experts on end-to-end work [B14]. Both show single-shot competence, not sustained monotonic improvement — no public benchmark yet measures "grind a fixed metric upward for days without cheating," which is Ascent's claimed lane.
8. **The AI Scientist family (Sakana):** AI Scientist-v2 produced a fully autonomous paper that passed ICLR 2025 workshop review (later voluntarily withdrawn), at ~$20–25 total API cost per paper [B15]. This legitimizes agentic research and confirms the brief's positioning: Sakana optimizes for *novel one-shot papers on cloud budgets*; nobody in that family grinds a user's fixed real-world metric monotonically with audit gates.
9. **Karpathy autoresearch (verified):** released Mar 7 2026; 54k GitHub stars in 19 days (faster than nanoGPT's 3-year run); ~86k stars / ~12.5k forks by Jun 2026 — and, notably, **no active maintainer** as of Jun 2026 [B29][B30]. The brief's "78k+ stars, 11k+ forks" is consistent with (slightly behind) the Jun 2026 figures. The demand signal is real; the maintenance vacuum strengthens the case that a productized rigor layer is the unfilled gap.

## 3. LLM API cost curves — and what a 24/7 steering agent costs

10. **Cost per unit of capability is collapsing.** GPT-4-class quality: ~$30/Mtok input at launch (Mar 2023) → ~$0.50 by early 2026 → ~$0.14/Mtok (DeepSeek V4-Flash, Jul 2026), ≈214× in 40 months [B17]. Epoch AI: prices at fixed benchmark performance fall 9–900×/yr depending on task (median ~50×/yr; ~40×/yr on PhD-science questions), accelerating post-2024 [B16].
11. **Representative list prices, Aug 2026 (per Mtok in/out):** Anthropic — Fable 5 $10/$50, Opus 5 $5/$25, Sonnet 5 $2/$10 (promo through Aug 31 2026, then $3/$15), Haiku 4.5 $1/$5; batch API −50%, cache hits at 10% of input price [B18]. OpenAI — gpt-5.6-sol $5/$30, gpt-5.6-terra $2/$12, gpt-5.6-luna $0.20/$1.20, gpt-5-nano $0.05/$0.40 [B19]. Google — Gemini 3.7 Flash $0.75/$3.75 (through Dec 2026; doubling to $1.50/$7.50 on Jan 1 2027), Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite $0.10/$0.40 [B20][B21]. For 2024 comparison: GPT-5 launched Aug 2025 at $1.25/$10; GPT-4-class was $30/Mtok input in 2023 [B17][B19].
12. **Important nuance that partially contradicts the brief:** *frontier-tier* list prices are flat-to-**rising** — OpenAI doubled GPT-5-line prices with GPT-5.5 (Apr 23 2026: $2.50→$5 in, $15→$30 out), Google has a scheduled Gemini Flash price doubling (Jan 2027), and Anthropic's newest flagship (Fable 5, $10/$50) costs 2× its predecessor tier [B19][B21][B18]. The falling-cost story is true only as *cost per unit of capability* (yesterday's frontier gets cheap fast). The brief's why-now #3 should be phrased that way.
13. **24/7 steering-agent cost (computed, clearly an estimate).** A continuous steering loop consuming ~2–5M fresh input + ~0.5–1M output tokens/day with prompt caching: ≈ $20–60/day on Sonnet-5-tier, ≈ $3–12/day on Haiku 4.5 / Gemini 3.7 Flash tier, < $2/day on luna/nano/Flash-Lite tier. Observed anchors: average Claude Code developer spend is ~$6/day with 90% of users under ~$12–30/day (interactive use) [B22]; heavy-automation power users report $500–2,000/month ≈ $17–65/day [B23]. Every point in that range is under 10% of a $150k+/yr ML engineer's ~$600 fully-loaded working-day cost — the economic claim in the brief's business model holds with an order of magnitude of headroom, *provided* the loop routes routine steps to mid-tier models.

## 4. Literature-retrieval tooling

14. **Programmatic literature access is commodity infrastructure in 2026.** Semantic Scholar Graph API, OpenAlex (free, no API key, generous rate limits), arXiv API, Crossref REST, and PubMed E-utilities jointly expose 300M+ scholarly records; standard practice combines them (Crossref for DOIs, OpenAlex for citations/affiliations, Semantic Scholar for abstracts/embeddings) [B24]. Off-the-shelf agent-skill suites already wrap OpenAlex for search, citation-chasing, and two-pass literature screening [B25]; toolkits like LitLLM build review pipelines on the Semantic Scholar API [B24a].
15. **Implication for Ascent:** the "Literature-ground" step and the citation-rigor audit gate can be built on free, stable, rate-limit-generous infrastructure. Retrieval is not a moat for anyone; *enforced correct use* of retrieval (no hallucinated citations passes the gate) is where Ascent adds value.

## 5. Consumer hardware — what fits on a 2026 laptop

16. **Unified memory reached laptop scale.** The M5 Max MacBook Pro (shipped Mar 2026) offers 128 GB unified memory at 614 GB/s with per-GPU-core neural accelerators — it runs 90B-parameter LLMs locally, and (after Apple cut the Mac Studio to 96 GB) is the highest-memory Mac sold [B26]. AMD Strix Halo mini-PCs offer the same 128 GB capacity at roughly one-third the price at under half the bandwidth [B26].
17. **Training envelope:** 7B-parameter QLoRA fine-tunes need ~16–20 GB (fits mid-tier laptops); 70B fine-tunes are feasible on the 128 GB config with gradient checkpointing [B27]. On tabular workloads — Ascent's validated domains — LightGBM/XGBoost-hist train multi-million-row GBM models in minutes on ordinary laptop CPUs, with LightGBM ~10–20× faster than classic XGBoost at that scale [B28].
18. **Implication:** the entire evidence base of Ascent's PoC (fraud detection, EUR/USD Sharpe, Higgs — GBM/tabular/small-model scale) sits comfortably inside a 2026 laptop's envelope, and nanochat-scale LLM training (the autoresearch demo domain) sits inside a single consumer GPU. "Laptop-native research hardware" is a 2026 fact, not a projection — bounded above by frontier-scale pretraining, which Ascent explicitly does not target.

## Why now, in three sourced sentences

1. Autonomous-agent task horizons doubled every ~4 months since 2024, reaching ~12 hours at 50% reliability by mid-2026 (vs under an hour in early 2025) — the multi-hour literature-grounded experiment loop Ascent chains together simply did not exist as a dependable capability 18 months ago [B1][B2][B5].
2. Agents now win medals on ~40–76% of Kaggle-style ML tasks (vs 17% in Oct 2024) and beat human experts 4× on 2-hour research-engineering sprints, yet still lose 2× to humans at 32-hour budgets — proving the execution engine works and that encoded research judgment (Ascent's constitution) is exactly the missing layer [B4][B11][B13].
3. Cost per unit of model capability is falling ~40–50× per year, putting a 24/7 mid-tier steering agent at roughly $3–60/day — under 10% of one ML engineer's daily cost — while 128 GB unified-memory laptops and minutes-scale GBM training make the experiment substrate free at the margin [B16][B17][B18][B22][B26][B28].
